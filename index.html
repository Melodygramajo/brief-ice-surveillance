Measurement has to fit in minutes

The ≥355 ms lapse threshold for the 3-minute PVT-B was validated on dedicated laboratory hardware with near-zero latency [2]. A standard browser and screen add roughly 80–120 ms of input and display latency, which inflates reaction times above 355 ms. Browser responses therefore cannot be judged against the raw laboratory threshold without device calibration. Demonstration only; nothing is stored or sent.

Process S builds while awake and falls during sleep; Process C sets a daily window. Shorten three nights and watch S cross the upper threshold during waking hours.

Didactic implementation with textbook-style parameters [3]; not fitted to any person or dataset and not a predictive or clinical tool. The classic exponential Process S has no memory and stabilizes within 2–3 nights of partial sleep restriction, yet empirical data show PVT lapses keep accumulating over weeks of restricted sleep [4]. That gap is one reason the project favors longitudinal, multi-day monitoring.

The review

What it will answer

Which brief sleep and vigilance measures have been used in isolated, confined and extreme environments, and does the evidence support using them to detect change against a person's own baseline?

  1. Mapping

    Which measures, how long, how often, on what device and in which setting.

  2. Repeated use

    Test–retest stability, practice effects and sensitivity to sleep loss.

  3. Level of analysis

    How many studies look at individual change instead of group averages only.

  4. Representation

    Who was studied, and who is missing.

Method

Three phases, in order

Phase 1

PRISMA-ScR scoping review

Core
  • Protocol registration on OSF
  • PubMed, Scopus, IEEE Xplore, Web of Science, NASA NTRS, plus LILACS and SciELO, with no language restriction
  • Dual independent screening in Rayyan, consensus on disagreements

Phase 2

Benchmarking on analog data

Conditional on data access
  • Audit of NASA OSDR (incorporating ALSDA records) and the NASA Life Sciences Data Archive (LSDA, for controlled-access human analog datasets)
  • Formal data request where access is restricted

Phase 3

Minimal Dataset

Core
  • Core variables for spaceflight and terrestrial use
  • Low-resource terrestrial transferability
  • Offline-first implementation specification
